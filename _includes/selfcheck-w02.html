Four questions on market definition, HHI, density, and sandbox / corridor interpretation. Keep Sandbox ≠ Sandbox X; corridor policy / trial ≠ day-to-day operating service.

1. Before talking about “competition in LAE,” these notes first ask you to…
Learning objective 1; Study checklist 1–2; §2–3. Four segments (logistics, air taxis, inspection, EMS) have different competitive logics. HHI is a screen on a defined market, not a city-wide LAE score. Sandbox counts are programme facts, not shares.
2. The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) in Week 2 is…
Named formula Formulas · Week 2 · HHI; Study checklist 3; §4. Higher HHI means more uneven shares. It does not prove market power alone. ATC and density are separate tools (§§5–6).
3. Economies of density (Week 2) mean…
Learning objective 2; Study checklist 5; §6; Formulas · density. Density means more volume on the same corridor and sites. Bigger vehicles describe scale; wider networks may share other costs; lower fares are a pricing result, not a reduction in production cost.
4. Which statement matches the Week 2 sandbox / corridor guards?
Study checklist 10–11; §8 Absolute programme facts. Both programmes have a count of 38, but they measure different selections; neither count is a market share or HHI input. A policy or trial corridor is not proof of routine commercial service.
