Five questions from Week 1 learning objectives, the Study checklist, and named formulas only. Keep the standing guards: Sandbox ≠ Sandbox X; OHKF HK$350bn = industry value added; ~1,000 m ≠ Cap. 448G flying limit; U-space ≠ Hong Kong law.

1. In these notes, the low-altitude economy (LAE) is best defined as…
LAE is framed as a mission / service system (Learning objective 1; Study checklist 1; §1). “Flying cars only” misses logistics, inspection, EMS, and supporting services. Cap. 448G is Hong Kong’s SUA frame, not the definition of LAE. U-space is an EU / industry frame — not Hong Kong law.
2. Hong Kong policy language often describes LAE as activity generally below about 1,000 m. That figure is…
Keep the layers separate (Study checklist 2; §1 Policy band vs flying limits). ~1,000 m is policy framing of the economic field. Cap. 448G ordinary bands are 100 ft (A1) and 300 ft ≈ 91 m (A2) AGL. Mainland height labels are a different scheme.
3. When these notes quote “about HK$350 billion,” the correct attribution is…
Attribution rule (§5; Study checklist 11): name OHKF, metric (industry value added), geography (Hong Kong), horizon (2050). Value added ≠ revenue. Not a government target; not Mainland scale totals.
4. Which statement about Hong Kong sandboxes is correct?
Learning objective 5; Study checklist 8–9; §§10–11. Sandbox ≠ Sandbox X. A sandbox changes economic entry frictions; it is not a safety waiver. Cite first-batch and Sandbox X counts separately (Key figures).
5. Generalized cost (short form) in Week 1 is…
Named formula on Formulas · Week 1 · GC and §7. GC puts money and time on one scale; Pmax follows from matching an alternative’s GC (§8 AE ceiling illustration). U-space is not Hong Kong law; NPV is a separate viability screen (− initial investment + present value of future profits).
