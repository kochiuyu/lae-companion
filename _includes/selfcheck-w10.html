Four questions from Week 10 learning objectives, the Study checklist, and named formulas (MECnoise, Cpriv, eop, eFU). “Electric” ≠ zero life-cycle emissions, quietness, privacy, or equity. Sandbox ≠ Sandbox X.

1. An externality in Week 10 is…
Learning objective 1; Study checklist 1–2; §2. An externality falls on people outside the transaction: noise is a possible cost; faster emergency response may create benefits beyond the paying customer. Private operating costs and CASK gaps are not externalities by themselves. Policy responses include pricing, restrictions, standards, mitigation, and compensation.
2. The efficient noise-charge benchmark C*noise = MECnoise means…
Study checklist 3–4, 6; Formulas · noise. Marginal external noise cost depends on who is exposed, when, where, and how; decibels are logarithmic, so “flights × dB × $” is not a valid shortcut. The benchmark is not a published CAD tariff, and a classroom carbon value is a separate appraisal input.
3. Operational electricity emissions eop are…
Learning objective 3; Study checklist 7–8; Formulas · eop and eFU. Operational electricity emissions equal kWh consumed times a sourced electricity factor. Manufacturing and infrastructure belong in a wider life-cycle boundary; hold the functional unit and boundary constant when comparing modes.
4. Privacy-control cost Cpriv = Fgov + q v + A…
Learning objective 4–5; Study checklist 5, 9–12; Formulas · Cpriv. Fgov is fixed governance cost, qv scales with use, and A covers audit and incident cost. Apply necessity and proportionality to the chosen controls, then set measurable KPIs with owners. Public acceptance still varies by mission and affected community.
