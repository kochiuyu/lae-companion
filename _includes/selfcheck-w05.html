Four questions from Week 5 learning objectives, the Study checklist, and named formulas (generalized site cost, reliable capacity, breakeven capacity use). No dedicated Hong Kong vertiport statute in these notes; Sandbox ≠ Sandbox X.

1. For a named mission, Week 5 first asks you to identify…
Learning objective 1–2; Study checklist 1–2, 5, 12; §§1–3. Start with the resource that binds the actual mission; theoretical pad throughput alone can hide aircraft, charging, airspace, or access limits. Classroom archetypes are teaching labels, not Hong Kong statutory classes, and sandbox counts are not capacity.
2. Generalized site cost in Week 5 includes…
Study checklist 3–4; Formulas · generalized site cost. Include annualized infrastructure and operating costs, and convert customer access time into money before adding it. Cheap remote land can worsen Week 4 mode share; a lower cost score does not override safety or service requirements.
3. Reliable capacity vs theoretical throughput means…
Learning objective 3; Study checklist 5–8; §§4–6. Reliable capacity accounts for operating hours, turnaround, maintenance, weather, and priority access. Downtime examples are classroom stresses, not a fixed Hong Kong weather fact; requested slots above safe capacity cannot be counted as served.
4. Hub vs distributed network design in these notes is…
Learning objective 5; Study checklist 11–12; § network design. Compare hub and distributed designs as route-specific hypotheses using served trips, access, site cost, and reliability. A cheap build or a broad value-added projection does not settle the network choice; EASA, Shenzhen, and OHKF are different kinds of comparators, not a Hong Kong legal template.
